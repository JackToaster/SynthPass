Here's an idea I've had bouncing around my head for a while, I want someone to tell me why it won't work.
What if we make a standard protogen boop module? It could be some very simple wireless module that can detect other nearby modules and transfer some pre-defined data format once it's within booping range of another protogen with the module (say, a few inches). The boop module would just plug into a UART on the protogen and have a super simple serial protocol that's easy to integrate with protogen firmware. Make example  code or even an Arduino library, prototracer integration, etc so it's as easy as possible to integrate.
Now the main challenge would be making it widespread enough that you'll actually run into other proots with the boop module. This is where the magic of modern electronics manufacturing comes in, it can be easily made for <<$1, so my idea is to make dozens/hundreds of them and give them out for free to protogens and protogen makers, along with instructions on how to implement it.

What I've been thinking is to have them send an advertisement (broadcast every few seconds or so) with a uuid and (optionally) name, then a response with the uuid of the server/responder and RSSI. Once they get a response they would ping each other more frequently with RSSI to detect range, probably a few Hz. Once both sent & received RSSI are high enough one or the other would send the "boop" packet, with all the name/contact info/boop parameters. Then there'd be an "end boop" packet too when the RSSI goes low enough.
I think it's important to control what's in the broadcast/boop packets so you can control whether your name/contact info is available for anyone you're near or just people you boop
